Blocked Drain in Dubai? What It Costs to Clear in 2026 — and What Your Drain Is Telling You

A blocked drain in Dubai rarely announces itself politely. The kitchen sink drains a little slower on Monday, gurgles on Wednesday, and by Friday the water is standing in the basin with a smell you cannot ignore. A bathroom drain does the same thing in slow motion: hair and soap build up for months, then one morning the shower tray is ankle-deep.

By Al Hamra Technical Services · Published 13 November 2026 · Reading time: ~8 minutes · Category: Plumbing · Dubai, UAE

The good news is that most blocked drains are small jobs. A standard blockage is usually cleared in 30 to 60 minutes with a mechanical snake, and the 2026 market bands in Dubai are clear and consistent: roughly AED 150–400 for a straightforward clear. The bad news is that the wrong fix — usually chemical drain cleaner poured in again and again — makes the real problem worse and hides it for longer.

What blocks a drain in Dubai

Dubai drains block for a handful of reasons, and knowing which one you have decides the fix — and the price.

Grease in the kitchen. Cooking oil and fat go down the sink as a liquid and set hard inside the pipe as they cool. Layer by layer, the pipe narrows until water cannot get through. This is the single most common cause of a kitchen drain blockage in Dubai, and it is also the most preventable: pour used oil into a container and bin it, and wipe greasy pans with a paper towel before washing them.

Hair and soap in the bathroom. Shower and basin drains collect hair, soap scum and the residue of shampoos and conditioners. The mixture mats together and catches on the pipe walls. A drain cover that is lifted and cleaned once a month stops most of it.

Sand and grit. Dubai is a dusty city. Fine sand and grit wash in through balcony and floor drains, settle in the traps, and build up where the pipe changes direction. Floor drain cleaning is a small job on its own — and one that matters most before the winter rain, when a blocked balcony drain can flood a balcony in one downpour.

The wrong things flushed down the toilet. A blocked toilet in Dubai is almost always caused by something that should never have been flushed: wipes (even the ones labelled flushable), sanitary products, cotton buds, or too much paper at once. Toilets are built to move water, paper and waste — nothing else.

Scale and age. Dubai's water is hard, and older buildings carry scale and corrosion inside their pipes. The pipe does not block in one day; it narrows over years until a normal amount of grease or hair is enough to close it. That is why a drain that blocks every few months is a different problem from a drain that blocks once — it usually means buildup on the pipe walls, not a one-off clog.

Two things in that table deserve a closer look.

A snake and a jetter are not the same job. A snake punches a hole through the blockage so water flows again. Hydrojetting scours the inside of the pipe clean, which is why it costs more — and why it lasts longer. For a drain that blocks every few months, jetting is usually the cheaper option over a year, because the blockage stops coming back. GoTopRated's clogged-drain guide (published 29 June 2026) makes the same point in money terms: a clogged drain costs AED 200–500 to clear in 2026, but the same blockage ignored for two months turns into a repair worth AED 1,500 — and a yearly professional drain service at AED 100–200 beats an emergency callout at AED 400 or more.

A camera is not an upsell — it is how you stop guessing. If a drain blocks repeatedly, or more than one fixture backs up, the cause is usually somewhere you cannot see: a cracked or collapsed pipe, a joint that has moved, or scale that has narrowed the line. A CCTV drain inspection in Dubai costs AED 400–700 on its own and is often cheaper when bundled with a cleaning job — and it is far cheaper than opening a wall or floor in the wrong place. What you can safely try at home — and what not to do

Before you call anyone, there are three safe checks:

  1. One honest plunge. A plunger works on a toilet or a sink when the blockage is close to the fixture. Use it properly — a good seal, steady pressure — and stop after a few attempts. If it has not moved, the blockage is further down the line and plunging harder will not reach it.
  2. Lift the drain cover and clean the trap. Most bathroom drains have a cover you can lift by hand. Pull out the hair and soap mat, rinse the cover, and put it back. Do this monthly and most bathroom blockages never happen.
  3. Flush with hot water — weekly, as prevention. A kettle of hot water down the kitchen sink once a week keeps fresh grease moving instead of setting in the pipe. It costs nothing and it works — but only as prevention. Hot water will not clear a blockage that has already set.

And three things not to do:

  • Do not keep pouring chemical drain cleaner down the drain. Chemical clearing is a temporary measure: it can dissolve enough of the blockage for water to flow, but it does not remove the obstruction, and the blockage returns — often faster the second time. It can also damage pipes and traps, and it makes the water sitting in the pipe hazardous for whoever has to work on it next. Pioneer-Gulf's guide is blunt about this: some companies use chemicals to clear a blockage temporarily rather than physically removing it with a snake or jetter, and the customer pays twice.
  • Do not open shared drain lines in a tower. If you live in an apartment tower, the drain stack serving several flats belongs to the building, not to your unit. Opening it or working on it without building management can affect your neighbours and your building's warranty. Tell building management, and let a plumber work on your unit's own lines.
  • Do not ignore a smell. A drain smell in Dubai is not just unpleasant — it is information. A dry trap (the U-bend under the sink has lost its water seal), a venting problem, or a blockage sitting and rotting in the pipe all smell different, and all of them get worse if they are left alone. Run water into every trap you rarely use — a guest bathroom, a floor drain — once a week, so the seal stays full.

When a blocked drain is a bigger problem

Call a plumber straight away — do not wait for it to get worse — if any of these three things is true:

More than one fixture backs up at once. The kitchen sink and the bathroom basin both draining slowly, or a toilet that gurgles when the washing machine drains, means the blockage is in a shared or main line, not in one fixture. That is a main sewer line cleaning job, and it needs a camera first to find where the line is blocked.

The same drain blocks every few months. A recurring blockage means buildup on the pipe walls, a displaced joint, or a pipe that has narrowed with scale. Snaking it again buys a few weeks. Jetting it, or finding the cause with a camera, fixes it.

Water is coming back up, or there is flooding. Water rising from a floor drain, or a toilet overflowing, is an active problem, not a slow one. Turn off the water to the affected fixture if you can, keep clear of the water, and call a plumber. If the water is near electrical points, do not touch them — and if a breaker trips, leave it off and read our guide to tripping breakers before touching anything.

The jobs that hide behind a blocked drain

A drain call is often how a bigger problem is found, and three of them are worth knowing about:

A badly connected appliance. Washing machines and dishwashers are two of the most common sources of a slow, hard-to-notice leak in Dubai homes — not because the appliances fail, but because the supply and drain connections behind them were rushed during installation. Tesla Cure's appliance-plumbing guide (published 15 October 2026) puts a standard hookup to an existing point at AED 150–300, a new point at AED 300–600, and a leak fix at an existing connection at AED 150–250. When our technicians clear a kitchen drain, they check the appliance connections behind it at the same time — a slow leak there can reach the flat below before anyone notices it.

A shower drain that is not a drain problem. Water appearing on the ceiling below a bathroom can be a blocked shower drain — or it can be a failed waterproofing membrane under the tiles. The two look identical from below and need different fixes; the short version is that a drain problem comes and goes with use, while a membrane failure leaves a stain that grows even when the shower is not running. Our guide to plumbing leaks and water pressure explains how to read the signs before anyone opens a ceiling.

A water bill that climbs for no reason. A drain fault wastes water differently from a leak — but an irrigation valve stuck open wastes it the same way a leak does, every night, unseen. If your DEWA bill has jumped and the drains are clear, read our post on why a DEWA bill is high in Dubai and check the irrigation controller before anything else. A valve stuck open is treated as urgent by villa maintenance companies, because of the water it wastes.

Frequently asked questions

How much does it cost to unblock a drain in Dubai?

A standard blocked drain costs AED 150–400 to clear with a mechanical snake in 2026, according to Tesla Cure (22 October 2026) and Home Ease Repairs (updated 17 October 2026). A moderate blockage runs AED 300–600, hydrojetting a residential line AED 500–900, and a CCTV camera inspection AED 400–700. The final price always depends on an inspection.

What causes drains to block in Dubai?

Grease in kitchen drains, hair and soap in bathroom drains, sand and grit in floor and balcony drains, and wipes or sanitary products flushed down toilets. In older buildings, scale narrowing the pipe is often the underlying cause of recurring blockages.

Is chemical drain cleaner safe to use?

It can clear enough of a blockage for water to flow, but it does not remove the obstruction, it can damage pipes and traps, and the blockage usually returns faster. A mechanical snake or hydrojetting physically removes the blockage. Do not use chemical cleaner repeatedly on the same drain.

When should I call a plumber instead of trying it myself?

If one honest attempt with a plunger has not worked, if more than one fixture is backing up at the same time, if the same drain blocks every few months, or if water is coming back up through a floor drain or toilet. Those are main-line or pipe-wall problems, not single-drain problems.

How do I stop my drains blocking again?

Bin cooking oil instead of pouring it down the sink, clean hair from shower drain covers monthly, run hot water down the kitchen sink weekly, run water into rarely used traps once a week so the water seal stays full, and book a professional drain service once a year — AED 100–200 a year beats an emergency callout at AED 400 or more.
