Water Filter Installation in Dubai: RO, Whole-House or Under-Sink — What It Costs in 2026

A filter in a Dubai home is not about safety — Dubai's tap water is desalinated and treated. It is about taste, chlorine, sediment and hardness, and about what a 5-gallon bottle costs you every week. Here are the three systems, which home each one suits, and what the 2026 prices actually are.

By Al Hamra Technical Services · Published 14 November 2026 · Reading time: ~12 minutes · Category: Plumbing & Water · Dubai, UAE

Dubai's tap water is desalinated and treated, and it is safe to drink. That is the first thing to say, because it is the question everyone asks — and the answer decides what a filter is actually for. A filter in a Dubai home is not about safety. It is about taste, chlorine, sediment and hardness: the flat taste of chlorine, the white scale that builds up on kettles and taps, and the grit that shortens the life of a washing machine and a water heater.

The second thing is the money. A home that drinks bottled water pays for it every week, all year. A filter is a one-off install plus a cartridge change a few times a year — and the 2026 market bands in Dubai are clear and consistent: roughly AED 400–900 for a basic under-sink filter installed, AED 1,200–2,500 for an under-sink reverse osmosis (RO) system installed, and AED 2,000–4,000 for a whole-house system in an apartment. This guide covers the three systems, which home each one suits, what the installation actually involves, what the running costs are, and the three mistakes to avoid. The three systems, in plain English

There are three systems worth knowing about, and they do different jobs. Buying the wrong one is the most common mistake in this category.

Under-sink two-stage filter — the simple one. A sediment cartridge catches sand and grit, then a carbon cartridge takes out chlorine taste and smell. That is all it does — it does not reduce dissolved solids, and it does not soften the water. It is the right choice when the complaint is taste and smell, and nothing else. It fits inside the kitchen cabinet, it is the cheapest to buy and the cheapest to maintain, and it is the easiest install.

Under-sink reverse osmosis (RO) — the drinking-water one. RO pushes water through a membrane that removes dissolved solids, heavy metals, nitrates and fluoride, as well as chlorine and sediment. It is the system for drinking and cooking water, and it is the one that replaces bottled water. It needs a dedicated filtered-water tap on the sink, a drain connection (the membrane produces waste water), and enough pressure to work — a correctly installed under-sink RO runs at 50 to 80 PSI, with airtight fittings. Dubai's desalinated water has a relatively low TDS (total dissolved solids, typically 100 to 200 ppm), which makes RO particularly effective here. The trade-off is space: the unit, its cartridges and usually a small tank all live under the sink, and the membrane needs replacing every two to three years.

Whole-house filtration — the every-tap one. This sits at the main supply where water enters the property, and it filters everything: every tap, every shower, the washing machine and the water heater. It protects appliances and pipework from sediment and scale as well as improving the water. It is the right choice for a villa, or for an apartment owner who wants the whole home covered. It is the wrong choice for a renter who cannot change the main supply — more on that below.

Two add-ons sit alongside these. A UV purifier is a lamp fitted inline after a filter; it kills bacteria and viruses with ultraviolet light. A water softener exchanges the hardness minerals (calcium and magnesium) for sodium, which stops scale forming on heaters and taps. A shower filter is a small cartridge on the shower head — the cheapest item in the category, from around AED 80, and the one that makes the most difference to skin and hair in hard water.

The five-year maths is the real price. It pays for itself over years, and it keeps paying after that.

Installation is not the same as buying the unit. Several of the prices above are "machine only" — the unit in a box. Installation is a separate job: the pressure check, the fittings, the drain connection, the dedicated tap, and the leak test. Fix On Click's note is the one to remember: a correctly installed under-sink RO operates at 50 to 80 PSI with airtight fittings, a proper drain connection and a dedicated filtered-water tap. If the pressure is too low, the membrane does not work properly. If the drain connection is wrong, the unit leaks — slowly, behind the kitchen cabinet, where nobody notices it until the cabinet base swells. Which system fits which home

An apartment, owned. An under-sink RO is the usual answer: it covers drinking and cooking water, it fits in the cabinet, and it does not touch the building's supply. A whole-house system is possible if the apartment's main supply is accessible and building management agrees — check first, because the main supply in a tower is not always inside the unit.

An apartment, rented. An under-sink filter or RO is usually fine, because it can be removed when you leave. A whole-house system is not, unless the landlord agrees in writing. Dubai Law No. 26 of 2007, Article 19, says a tenant may not carry out maintenance works without the landlord's permission and any licences the authorities require — and a whole-house filter is a change to the fixed plumbing. Ask first, in writing, and keep the answer. The same rule is why our guides always say to check the tenancy contract before booking work that changes the property.

A villa, owned. A whole-house system is the usual answer: it protects every tap and every appliance, including the water heater and the washing machine, and the main supply is yours to work on. Add a UV stage if the household wants it — AED 1,200–2,500 to add one to an existing whole-house system, according to Tesla Cure's UV guide. An under-sink RO can sit alongside it for drinking water, but it is not needed for every tap once the whole house is filtered.

A villa, rented. Same rule as the apartment: under-sink first, whole-house only with the landlord's written agreement. A villa's main supply is more accessible, which makes the job easier — but the permission question is the same.

Hard water is a different problem from taste. If the complaint is white scale on taps, kettles and shower heads, a carbon filter will not fix it — carbon takes out taste and smell, not hardness. That job needs a softener, or an RO unit for the drinking water. Say which problem you have when you book, because the wrong system is a wasted install.

What the installation actually involves

A water filter installation is a plumbing job, and it takes about an hour for a standard under-sink unit. Here is what happens, in order:

  1. The pressure check. The technician checks the water pressure at the kitchen tap. RO needs 50 to 80 PSI. If the pressure is too low, the membrane cannot push water through properly, and the unit underperforms from day one. Low pressure is a different job — our guide to plumbing leaks and water pressure covers it, and a booster pump and a filter solve different pressure problems, so do not confuse the two.
  2. The space check. The unit, its cartridges and usually a small tank all have to fit under the sink, with room to change the cartridges later. A cabinet full of cleaning products is the most common reason an install takes longer than expected.
  3. The connections. A feed is taken from the cold supply under the sink, a drain connection is made for the RO waste water, and a dedicated filtered-water tap is fitted on the sink. Every joint is checked for leaks before the cabinet is closed up.
  4. The first flush. The system is flushed through, the water is tested, and the cartridges are dated so the next change is not a guess.

After that, the running costs are small and predictable: pre-filters every six to twelve months, cartridges every three to six months depending on the system, a membrane every two to three years, and a UV lamp every year if one is fitted. Fix On Click's bands put a cartridge visit at AED 150–500, a membrane replacement at AED 200, and a complete 5-stage RO service at AED 350. Book the cartridge change when it is due — a filter that is never changed is worse than no filter, because a saturated carbon cartridge stops removing chlorine and starts releasing what it has collected.

What not to do

  • Do not fit RO without checking the pressure. Below 50 PSI, the membrane does not work properly, the flow slows to a trickle, and the unit gets blamed for a supply problem. Check first.
  • Do not skip the drain connection. An RO unit produces waste water, and it has to go somewhere. A unit fitted without a proper drain connection leaks — slowly, behind the cabinet, where it rots the base before anyone sees it.
  • Do not buy a whole-house system for a rented home without permission. The landlord's written agreement comes first. An under-sink unit that can be removed is the renter's answer.
  • Do not expect a filter to fix a high DEWA bill. A filter does not change how much water you use. A high bill with no change in usage is a leak, not a filter problem — read our post on why a DEWA bill is high in Dubai and check for a leak first. A filter and a leak are different jobs, and buying the first will not find the second.
  • Do not buy the cheapest unit and skip the install. The unit in a box is not a working filter. The pressure check, the fittings, the drain connection and the leak test are the job — and they are what make the difference between a filter that works for five years and one that leaks in five weeks.

Frequently asked questions

How much does water filter installation cost in Dubai?

A basic under-sink two-stage filter costs AED 400–900 installed, an under-sink RO system AED 1,200–2,500 installed, and a whole-house system AED 2,000–4,000 for an apartment or AED 4,500–9,000 for a villa with UV, according to Tesla Cure (17 October 2026) and Nadine Aqua (updated 30 October 2026). Installation is a separate job from the unit itself. The final price always depends on an inspection.

Is Dubai tap water safe to drink?

Dubai tap water is desalinated and treated. A filter is about taste, chlorine, sediment and hardness — not safety. If the complaint is taste and smell, a carbon filter is enough; if it is scale, you need a softener or RO.

Which is better: RO or a whole-house filter?

They do different jobs. RO is for drinking and cooking water at one tap; a whole-house filter covers every tap and protects appliances and pipework. Most apartments suit an under-sink RO; most villas suit a whole-house system, sometimes with an under-sink RO as well for drinking water.

Do I need landlord permission to install a water filter?

For an under-sink unit that can be removed, usually not — but check your contract. For a whole-house system, yes: it changes the fixed plumbing, and Dubai Law No. 26 of 2007, Article 19, says a tenant may not carry out maintenance works without the landlord's permission. Get it in writing first.

How often do water filter cartridges need changing?

Pre-filters every 6–12 months, cartridges every 3–6 months depending on the system, an RO membrane every 2–3 years, and a UV lamp every year if one is fitted. A cartridge visit costs AED 150–500, a membrane replacement AED 200, and a complete 5-stage RO service AED 350 (Fix On Click, updated 22 days ago).

Will a water filter lower my DEWA bill?

No. A filter does not change how much water you use. A high bill with no change in usage is usually a leak — read our post on why a DEWA bill is high in Dubai and check for a leak first.
