Why Is My DEWA Bill So High in Dubai? The 4 Maintenance Checks That Find the Cause (2026)

In November the cooling load has dropped, so a bill that is still high usually means a real fault — dirty filters, a failed seal, a hidden leak, or a water heater running when it should not be. Here is how to read the bill line by line, then run four maintenance checks before you complain to DEWA.

By Al Hamra Technical Services · Published 12 November 2026 · Reading time: ~9 minutes · Category: DEWA & Energy · Dubai, UAE

A high DEWA bill in Dubai is the kind of surprise nobody enjoys. The total jumps, the first instinct is to blame the meter — and sometimes the meter really is the problem. But most of the time, the bill is telling the truth about the home. Something inside it is using more electricity or water than it should.

November is the best month of the year to find out which one it is. In summer, a Dubai bill roughly doubles, and a high total proves nothing — the air conditioning is doing the work. In November the cooling load has dropped. If your bill is still high now, there is a real fault to find. This guide shows you how to read the bill line by line, then run four maintenance checks — the air conditioning, the seals, hidden leaks, and the water heater — before you complain to DEWA. First, read the bill before you blame it

A DEWA bill is not one number. It is several charges stacked together, and the total alone does not tell you what went wrong. Open DEWA Smart Living (the DEWA app) and look at the consumption history, not just the amount due. Compare this month with the same month last year, not with last month — seasonality explains most jumps.

Here is what the lines mean, using DEWA's published 2026 residential tariffs:

  • Electricity, by slab. The first 2,000 kWh in a month are charged at 23 fils per kWh; 2,001–4,000 kWh at 28 fils; 4,001–6,000 kWh at 32 fils; and anything above 6,000 kWh at 38 fils. Only the units inside each band pay that band's rate — crossing 2,000 kWh makes the extra units dearer, not the whole bill. A flat using 2,400 kWh in August pays the higher rate on 400 units, at the exact moment usage peaks.
  • Fuel surcharge. For September 2026 this was 6 fils per kWh of electricity and AED 1.10 per cubic metre of water. It changes month to month, so a bill can rise even when your usage does not.
  • Water, in cubic metres. Water has been billed in cubic metres since March 2025, so older gallon-based explanations online are out of date. Residential water costs AED 7.70 per cubic metre for the first 27 m³ in a month, AED 8.80 from 27 to 54 m³, and AED 10.12 above 54 m³.
  • Sewerage charge. Calculated on your water use, so a water leak pushes this line up too.
  • Housing fee. This confuses new tenants more than any other line. It is not a DEWA charge at all — it is a Dubai Municipality fee of 5% of your annual rent, split into 12 monthly instalments and collected through DEWA. A home rented for AED 120,000 a year carries a housing fee of AED 500 a month before a single unit of electricity or water is added. If the fee looks wrong, check the annual rental value linked to your account against your Ejari contract.
  • Meter and service charges, plus 5% VAT on the taxable lines.

What is a normal bill? As a rough guide, a one-bedroom flat typically runs AED 300–600 a month for electricity and water, before the 5% housing fee — and with the fee, most one-bedroom tenants land around AED 550–850. Summer bills run roughly double the winter ones. So if your November bill is still at summer levels, work through the four checks below. If the bill still makes no sense after that, DEWA's own Consumption Verification service is the right next step — it offers free self-diagnosis and a free scheduled call with DEWA, plus an onsite visit option. Do not open electrical panels or interfere with utility meters; meter inspection charges can apply when the meter turns out not to be faulty.

Check 1: the air conditioning — filters, coils and ducts

Air conditioning is the whole story behind most high DEWA bills in Dubai. From June to September it can run most of the day and much of the night, and consumption crosses into the dearer slabs at exactly the wrong moment. In November the load should have fallen. If it has not, the system is probably working harder than it needs to.

Start with the simplest cause: blocked filters. A filter choked with five months of desert dust restricts airflow, so the unit runs longer to reach the same temperature. DEWA's own cooling guidance recommends regular filter cleaning and a thermostat setting of 24°C or higher. Setting the thermostat to 18°C does not cool the room faster; it just keeps the compressor running longer. Curtains closed during daylight hours, especially on west-facing windows, do more for the bill than any gadget sold for that purpose.

Then look deeper, at the ducts. Ducted systems — common in villas, townhouses, penthouses and larger apartments — accumulate fine desert dust and sand in the duct network, and in humid conditions can develop mould that circulates through every room connected to the system. The signs are a musty smell from the vents even after the unit itself has been cleaned, visible dust blowing from vents, and rooms that never feel quite cool enough. AC duct cleaning Dubai is a distinct service from a routine unit service: specialised vacuum equipment extracts material from the whole duct length, followed by sanitisation of the duct interior. Published 2026 market bands put a studio or one-bedroom apartment at roughly AED 450–800, a two- to three-bedroom apartment at AED 700–1,400, a three- to four-bedroom villa at AED 1,200–2,500, and larger villas at AED 2,000–3,500 or more, with sanitising adding roughly AED 150–500. The published interval is every 12 to 18 months for most properties (some guides say every two to three years) — and a system that has not been cleaned since it was installed, or since a renovation, is overdue. One warning from the guides: a quote far below AED 350 for any residential property, or a clean done without HEPA vacuum or negative-pressure equipment, is a sign to walk away.

A winter visit is also the right moment for the rest of the system: coil cleaning on both sides, a drain-line flush and pan clean, a refrigerant pressure check (a leak search, not just a top-up), an electrical health check with capacitor values metered, and thermostat calibration. That is the winter service window — November to February — and it is covered in detail in our guide to winter AC maintenance in Dubai. If your AC is not cooling properly at all, start with our guide on why your AC is not cooling, and if the breaker keeps tripping, read our guide on tripping breakers first — a tripping breaker is a safety issue, not a bill issue.

Check 2: the seals — cooled air leaving through the gaps

The second check costs almost nothing and is the easiest to miss. Worn door and window seals let cooled air escape and hot air enter, so the AC runs longer for the same result. The same goes for a sliding balcony door that has gone stiff: fine desert dust settles into aluminium tracks and grinds against the rollers every time the door moves, until the door no longer closes fully. A door that does not close properly is a gap in the envelope of the home.

Walk the home on a warm afternoon and feel around the edges of windows, the balcony door and the main door. A draught on your hand, daylight visible around a closed door, or a seal that has gone hard and cracked are all worth fixing. Resealing a window or door frame takes a tube of general-purpose silicone sealant — about AED 15 at real Dubai supplier prices — and a slide-on under-door seal is about AED 99. The job is labour and access, not materials. If the sliding door itself is the problem, cleaning the track, adjusting or replacing the rollers and using the correct lubricant usually brings it back — one of the most satisfying repairs there is, because the difference is instant.

Check 3: hidden leaks — the water side of the bill

If the electricity side of the bill looks normal but the water side has jumped, look for a leak — and look in the places you cannot see. DEWA's July 2026 household guidance lists water stains and faults around tanks, pipes and household equipment as warning signs, and DEWA's high-water alert is a courtesy feature: it does not guarantee detection of every leak.

Start with the visible checks. Look under every sink and around the base of every toilet for water marks. Check the water tank and the pipes around it. Watch the water meter: with every tap closed, the meter should not move. A toilet that runs on silently after flushing, a tap that drips overnight, or a shower mixer that weeps can each waste far more water than anyone guesses — and because the sewerage charge is calculated on water use, a leak pushes two lines up at once.

Then consider the hidden ones. A concealed leak inside a wall or under a slab shows up as a damp patch, a ceiling stain in the flat below, paint bubbling, or a musty smell that cleaning never fixes. Finding it by opening walls at random is the expensive way: one exploratory opening costs roughly AED 400–800 (AED 1,500–4,000 in marble or custom tile) per attempt. Detection first is cheaper — acoustic detection runs roughly AED 400–700, thermal imaging AED 500–800, and a combined survey AED 600–1,000, with most residential jobs taking one to three hours. Winter is the best season for thermal imaging, because cooler outside temperatures create the contrast the cameras need. Our guide to plumbing leaks and water pressure covers the visible symptoms; a detection survey covers the ones you cannot see.

One more water-side check belongs here: the water tank. Dubai Municipality requires every water storage tank to be emptied, cleaned and disinfected at least every six months, and a tank that is overdue can harbour the sediment and contamination that affects the whole home's water. Our guide to water tank cleaning in Dubai explains the six-month rule, the fines, and who pays — it was drafted on 11 November and is awaiting approval, so it is not linked here until it is live.

Check 4: the water heater — running when it should not be

The fourth check is the one November exposes first. As the mornings cool, residents notice the shower is lukewarm — or not hot at all. A water heater not heating in Dubai is usually one of a short list of faults, and several of them also push the electricity bill up: a thermostat stuck closed keeps the element heating long past the set temperature; a thermostat that has drifted leaves the water lukewarm, so the heater runs longer; a scaled-up element heats slowly and wastes electricity doing it; and a failed thermal cut-out or a tripped breaker points at an electrical fault that needs a qualified technician, not a reset-and-hope.

The symptoms to note before you call anyone: no hot water at all; water that is hot, then cold, then hot again; water that takes far longer to heat than it used to (twenty minutes becoming forty-five is a failing element, not old age); a heater that trips the breaker; a leak from a pipe connection or the pressure-relief valve; or loud banging noises from sediment build-up. Published 2026 market bands give a sense of scale: diagnosis AED 150–250, a thermostat or thermal cut-out AED 150–300, a heating element AED 150–400, a flush or descaling service AED 180–350, and an annual service AED 250–400. A like-for-like replacement of a standard 50–80 litre storage heater runs roughly AED 600–1,400 including the unit, with larger villa units at AED 1,800–3,500 or more. The repair-or-replace rule the guides agree on: repair it if the unit is under four years old, the tank is sound, and the fault is a single component. Replace it if the tank body itself is leaking, the unit is eight to ten years old, or it is making loud banging noises from sediment.

If the heater trips the breaker, do not keep resetting it — read our guide on tripping breakers and get the circuit checked. And if the heater is part of a wider pattern — the AC struggling, the bill high, the water lukewarm — say so when you book: one visit can cover several of these checks at once.

If the cause is inside the home, the fix is usually smaller than the bill suggests. A filter clean, a reseal, a thermostat, a detection survey — each costs a fraction of a year of inflated bills. And if you are a tenant, check your contract before you pay: the standard market practice in Dubai is that the tenant covers minor repairs under roughly AED 500 (sometimes AED 1,000 for villas) and the landlord covers everything above it, under Law No. 26 of 2007 — but the contract is what governs, so read it first.

A cheaper bill starts with a cooler, tighter, drier home

A high DEWA bill in Dubai is rarely a mystery once you separate the lines and check the home in order. In November, with the summer load gone, a bill that is still high is the home telling you something: the AC is choked with dust, a seal has failed, a pipe is leaking where you cannot see it, or the water heater is running when it should not be. Find the cause first, fix that one thing properly, and the next bill is usually the proof.

Frequently asked questions

Why is my DEWA bill so high in November when the AC is barely on?

Because the cooling load has dropped, a high DEWA bill Dubai households receive in November usually points at a fault rather than the weather: dirty AC filters or ducts making the system work harder, a water heater with a failed thermostat running constantly, a hidden leak pushing up water use (and the sewerage charge with it), or a housing fee based on a rental value that does not match your Ejari contract. Work through the four checks above before assuming the meter is wrong.

How much does AC duct cleaning cost in Dubai?

Published 2026 market bands put a studio or one-bedroom apartment at roughly AED 450–800, a two- to three-bedroom apartment at AED 700–1,400, a three- to four-bedroom villa at AED 1,200–2,500, and larger villas at AED 2,000–3,500 or more, with sanitising adding roughly AED 150–500. The published interval is every 12 to 18 months for most properties. These are market bands from named 2026 guides, not Al Hamra's prices — the final price depends on inspection.

My water heater is not heating — should I repair or replace it?

Repair it if the unit is under four years old, the tank is sound, and the fault is a single component such as the element or thermostat (typically AED 150–400 in published 2026 bands). Replace it if the tank body itself is leaking, the unit is eight to ten years old, or it is making loud banging noises from sediment. A like-for-like replacement of a standard 50–80 litre storage heater runs roughly AED 600–1,400 including the unit.

Can Al Hamra check my DEWA meter or dispute my bill?

No. Only DEWA can verify a meter. Use DEWA's Consumption Verification service — self-diagnosis and a scheduled call are free — and do not open electrical panels or interfere with utility meters. Al Hamra checks the home: the AC, the seals, the leaks and the water heater.
