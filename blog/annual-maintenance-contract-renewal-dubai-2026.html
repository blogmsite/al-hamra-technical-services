Annual Maintenance Contract Renewal in Dubai (2026): Costs, Cover and What to Check Before You Sign

Your maintenance contract is up for renewal, and the price has gone up — or the cover has quietly shrunk. Before you sign for another year, here is what a Dubai AMC should cost in 2026, what it must cover, and the seven checks that separate a good contract from an expensive one.

If you own or rent a home in Dubai, there is one bill you will meet every year whether you plan for it or not: maintenance. The air conditioning runs ten to twelve months a year. The water is hard enough to eat through a heater element in a few years. A small leak left alone becomes a ceiling stain, then a ceiling repair. An annual maintenance contract — an AMC — is how most villa and apartment owners turn that unpredictable list into one fixed yearly cost. And if your contract is coming up for renewal, November is the month to get it right.

This guide explains what an annual maintenance contract renewal in Dubai should cost in 2026, what a good contract must cover, what is usually excluded, and the seven checks to make before you sign — for a villa or an apartment, with the published 2026 price bands so you can judge any quote against the market.

Price note: every price band in this guide is a published 2026 market band from a named Dubai source, labelled as such. None of these are Al Hamra’s prices — Al Hamra has supplied no prices, and every job is priced after inspection.

Why November is renewal season

Most Dubai maintenance contracts run for twelve months, and a large share of them were signed in the quiet winter months. That makes October to December the renewal window — and the most negotiable window of the year. Top Fixers, one of Dubai’s larger operators, notes that pricing is negotiable in the October–December period because maintenance companies are competing for annual renewals, and it reviewed its own AMC prices as recently as 9 November 2026. European Technical, another Dubai provider, expanded its AMC services in July 2026 after its waiting list for new contracts grew by 40% in the first half of the year, and reports that around 70% of its 2026 AMC signups are first-time buyers who previously relied on informal, call-on-demand repairs.

The timing is not an accident. November is also the best month for the work itself: the summer cooling load is over, technicians are available, and a November visit catches the wear summer left behind — fouled coils, blocked drains, tired capacitors, slow gas leaks — before the next cooling season. A contract signed or renewed now buys you the quiet-season service first, then the pre-summer visit in March, then the mid-summer check when the system is working hardest.

There is a second reason renewals cluster now. Dubai is handing over homes at a pace not seen in five years: more than 18,000 residential units were handed over in the first quarter of 2026 alone, a 27% rise on the same period last year, and a property consultancy survey found 63% of new villa owners commission at least one maintenance service within 60 days of handover. New owners arriving in the winter months are exactly the households shopping for their first AMC — which is why the renewal and first-contract decisions are being made in the same weeks.

That is the maths a renewal decision is really about — not the annual fee, but the one repair it prevents.

AC maintenance contract cost in Dubai: what the AC part should include

The air conditioning is the heart of every Dubai AMC, so it deserves its own check. AC maintenance contract cost in Dubai is the first price most shoppers ask about, and the answer depends on how many visits the contract promises. Most residential contracts include two or four visits a year. Quarterly servicing — every three months — is the cadence the 2026 guides agree on for Dubai: one visit before summer to prepare the system, one mid-summer check, and two visits in the cooler months for general upkeep. Contracts with only two visits cost less but leave a bigger gap during the months when the AC is under the most stress.

A proper AC visit should take 45 to 75 minutes per unit and should include filter cleaning, coil cleaning, a refrigerant check and drain clearing — with a chemical coil clean at least once a year, not just a filter rinse. The November visit matters more than most owners realise: Dubai split units need a full professional clean every three months during the April–October cooling season, plus one more service in November before reduced-load winter operation. Skip it, and the evidence is measurable — a visibly fouled coil raises electricity consumption by 18 to 25% (Good Men Project).

Watch the add-ons. A single gas refill can run AED 200–500 depending on refrigerant type, and duct cleaning AED 400–1,200 depending on the length of the duct run. Some contracts include one chemical service a year; some charge it as an extra of AED 300–500 per unit. Ask which it is before you renew, not after the first visit. What a good AMC must cover

A contract that only covers the AC is an AC plan, not a home maintenance contract. Before you renew, check that these are in writing:

  • AC: two to four scheduled services a year, priority response for breakdowns, and a stated position on gas top-ups and chemical cleans.
  • Plumbing: annual inspection, leak checks, tap and valve repairs, drain clearing — and a water-heater service. December is the water-heater servicing month in Dubai’s maintenance calendar, and hard desalinated water is the reason: elements last four to eight years here, and an annual service at AED 200–350 compares with AED 1,200–2,500 for a new heater. If you have ever had no hot water on a winter morning, the heater service is the part of the contract that earns its fee. See the SKN water heater repair bands and the GeeM water heater leaking guide (published 6 Nov 2026). Al Hamra’s plumbing guide on the homepage guides covers leaks, drips and low pressure.
  • Electrical: annual safety inspection, distribution board check, socket and switch repairs, and fault finding. If a breaker keeps tripping, that is a contract job, not a call-out — Al Hamra’s guide to tripping breakers explains what the usual causes are (see the guides on the homepage).
  • Water tank cleaning: Dubai Municipality requires potable water tanks to be cleaned at least every six months, with a compliance certificate issued each time. A whole-property AMC should include or schedule both cleans — a villa owner who misses them is not just risking water quality, the requirement is a legal minimum, and non-compliance can draw fines. See European Technical on water tank cleaning.
  • General repairs and handyman hours: door and lock adjustments, wardrobe and cabinet fixes, minor painting touch-ups and grout work. The small jobs are the ones that pile up between visits, and a contract with a stated pool of handyman hours each quarter is worth more than one that charges every small job as a call-out.
  • Emergency call-outs: how many are included, what hours they cover, and what the response commitment actually is. “Unlimited emergency call-outs” means little without a response time in writing.

What is usually NOT covered — read this before you renew

Most renewal disputes come from the exclusions, not the price. In the published 2026 contract guides, these are the items most commonly charged separately:

  • Spare parts and refrigerant. Most contracts cover labour and charge parts at cost — Top Fixers, for example, supplies parts at cost with a 15% contract discount. A contract that claims to include all parts for a villa-wide fee is either very expensive or very narrow in practice.
  • Major replacements. A failed compressor, a new water heater, a replacement AC unit — these are quoted separately. Know the threshold: some contracts state a per-job parts cap, and anything above it is a separate quote.
  • Work outside the home. Common-area systems, building chilled-water plant and district cooling are the building’s responsibility, not your AMC’s.
  • Damage you caused, or that was already there. Pre-existing faults found on the first inspection are usually excluded or quoted separately — which is why the first visit of a renewed contract should include a full inspection, and why new-villa owners should keep their snagging list. Under Dubai’s Law No. 6 of 2019, the developer stays liable for ten years for structural defects and one year for defective installations, so a defect found in year one belongs to the developer, not to your maintenance contract. See the Pearlshire snagging guide.
  • Painting and renovation. A full repaint is a separate job. Touch-ups may be included; a move-out repaint is not — and if you are moving out, the repaint is a deposit question first and a maintenance question second. Published 2026 bands put a move-out repaint at AED 400–700 for a studio, AED 899–1,999 for a two-bedroom apartment and AED 1,800–4,000 for a small villa (TaskRight move-out painting costs). Al Hamra’s repainting guide is on the homepage guides.

Villa vs apartment: which contract fits which home

The renewal decision is different by property type, and the villa maintenance cost Dubai 2026 figures show why. A typical four-bedroom villa owner should budget around AED 6,000–8,000 a year for maintenance, according to the Brothers Forever guide written from more than 500 villa jobs — older villas need more, newer ones less. A villa has more of everything: four to ten AC units instead of one or two, a water tank on the roof, a pump, more bathrooms, more doors, more grout, and often a pool and garden. The whole-property AMC is the natural fit, because the alternative — calling a different technician for every trade — is how small problems become expensive ones.

An apartment has fewer systems and a building management team behind the common areas. For many apartments, an AC-only plan plus a pay-as-you-go handyman is enough — the European Technical bands put AC-only apartment cover at AED 450–900 a year for a studio or one-bedroom, against AED 900–2,800 for whole-apartment cover. The question to ask is not “which is cheaper” but “what breaks in my flat?” If the answer is mostly the AC and the odd tap, the AC plan wins. If the answer is the AC, the heater, the drains and a door that will not close, the whole-apartment plan is cheaper by the second visit.

For tenants, one more check: who pays? Under Dubai Law No. 26 of 2007, the landlord maintains the property and fixes defects that affect the tenant’s use of it, unless the contract says otherwise — and in practice most contracts put repairs under AED 500 on the tenant and everything above it on the landlord. A tenant should not be renewing a landlord’s AMC, and a landlord should not be asking them to. Check the Ejari-registered contract before anyone signs. Seven things to check before you sign or renew

  1. Visits in writing. How many scheduled visits, in which months? Two visits a year leaves a gap in the cooling season; four is the Dubai standard for homes that run the AC six or more hours a day.
  2. What a visit includes. Filter rinse only, or coil clean, refrigerant check and drain clear? One chemical clean a year should be in the price.
  3. Response time in writing. “Priority response” is a promise; “within four hours” is a term. Ask for the number.
  4. Parts and exclusions. What is covered, what is charged at cost, and what is quoted separately? Get the per-job cap if there is one.
  5. The renewal price, not the first-year price. Some providers discount year one and raise year two. Ask for the renewal figure before the first year ends — and remember October to December is the window when that figure is negotiable.
  6. VAT and the quote format. Confirm whether the annual fee includes 5% VAT, and insist on a written quote that separates labour, parts, visits and exclusions before any work starts.
  7. Who does the work. One company with its own technicians, or a job passed to a subcontractor? A contract is only as good as the technician who turns up, and a renewal is the moment to judge the last twelve months honestly: did they turn up on time, did they fix it first time, and did the bill match the quote?

When an AMC does not make sense

An honest guide says so. If your home is a studio with one AC unit, a new building under a developer warranty, and nothing else that breaks, paying per visit may cost less than a contract — a single split service runs AED 100–300 a visit in the published 2026 bands, so two visits a year can be cheaper than the cheapest plan. If you are about to move out, do not renew: spend the money on the pre-move-out repair bundle instead — a AED 1,000–1,500 bundle of painting, door realignment and grout work before the inspection routinely saves two to three times that in deposit deductions billed at the landlord’s contractor rates (Real Estate Club Dubai handyman prices 2026). And if your current provider missed visits, charged for work the contract covered, or took days to answer an emergency, renewing with them because it is easier is the most expensive option of all.

The renewal decision, in one paragraph

An annual maintenance contract renewal in Dubai is worth signing when the price sits inside the published 2026 bands for your property type, the contract promises four AC visits a year with a chemical clean, covers plumbing, electrical and the water heater, schedules the six-monthly tank clean, states its response time and its exclusions in writing, and costs less than the one repair it is most likely to prevent. November is the month to make that judgement — the renewal window is open, the prices are negotiable, the technicians are free, and the summer’s wear is still in the unit waiting to be found. Book the inspection, read the exclusions, and renew the contract that earns its fee.

Frequently asked questions

How much does an annual maintenance contract cost in Dubai in 2026?

Published 2026 market bands: AC-only apartment cover AED 450–1,500 a year depending on size; whole-apartment cover AED 900–2,800; a three-bedroom villa AED 1,500–6,000 depending on whether the plan is AC-led or whole-property; a four-bedroom villa AED 2,500–7,500; a large villa AED 4,000–14,000. These are market bands from named Dubai guides, not Al Hamra’s prices — final price always depends on inspection.

When is the best time to renew an AMC in Dubai?

October to December. Providers compete for annual renewals in this window, so pricing is negotiable, and a November renewal buys the quiet-season service first — the post-summer check that catches the wear the cooling season left behind.

What should an AMC cover in Dubai?

At minimum: two to four scheduled AC services a year with a chemical clean, plumbing inspection and leak checks, electrical safety inspection, water-heater service, six-monthly water tank cleaning with a compliance certificate, a pool of handyman hours, and stated emergency response. Parts are usually charged separately at cost.

Is an AMC worth it for an apartment?

Often yes for two- and three-bedroom apartments with several AC units; for a studio with one unit, paying per visit (AED 100–300 per split service in published 2026 bands) can be cheaper. Compare the annual fee with the cost of the visits and the one repair you would otherwise pay for.

Does an AMC cover the water heater and water tank?

A whole-property contract should. The water heater needs an annual service (AED 200–350 in published bands) because Dubai’s hard water shortens element life, and Dubai Municipality requires potable water tanks to be cleaned at least every six months. Check both are named in the contract, not assumed.

Who pays for the AMC — tenant or landlord?

Under Dubai Law No. 26 of 2007 the landlord maintains the property unless the Ejari-registered contract says otherwise. In practice tenants usually cover repairs under AED 500 and landlords cover everything above that. The AMC itself is normally the owner’s cost — check your contract before signing.
