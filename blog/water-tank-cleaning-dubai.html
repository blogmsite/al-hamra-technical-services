Water Tank Cleaning in Dubai (2026): the 6-Month Rule, Fines up to AED 50,000, and Who Pays

Dubai Municipality requires every water storage tank to be emptied, cleaned and disinfected at least every six months — by an approved contractor, with a certificate kept on file. If the last cleaning certificate is more than six months old, the fine can reach AED 50,000.

By Al Hamra Technical Services · Published 13 November 2026 · Reading time: ~13 minutes · Category: Plumbing & Water · Dubai, UAE

Every home in Dubai stores its water in a tank — on the roof of a villa, or in the basement and on the roof of a tower. The water that comes out of your taps, your shower and your kitchen has been sitting in that tank. And in Dubai, how often that tank is cleaned is not a matter of preference. It is a rule, with a fine attached, and most residents have never heard of it.

Dubai Municipality requires every water storage tank to be emptied, cleaned and disinfected at least every six months. Not once a year. Not "when it looks dirty". Every six months, by an approved contractor, with a certificate kept on file. If the last cleaning certificate is more than six months old, the fine can reach AED 50,000.

This guide explains what water tank cleaning in Dubai actually involves in 2026, what it costs, who is responsible for paying — owner, landlord or tenant — and why November is the right month to get it done.

What Dubai Municipality actually requires

The rule sits in Dubai Municipality's Technical Guidelines for Quality of Unbottled Drinking Water (DM-HSD-GU17-DW2, issued 25 September 2024). In plain language, it says:

  • Every water tank must be emptied, cleaned and disinfected at least twice a year — a minimum of every six months. The clock starts from the date of the last cleaning certificate.
  • The cleaning must be done by a company approved by Dubai Municipality. The cleaning firm must hold approval from the Municipality's Food Safety Department, Registration and Permit Section. Ask for the establishment number before you book.
  • Disinfection uses chlorine at 50 mg/L, and after cleaning the water must be tested by an accredited laboratory — the lab report should confirm a free chlorine residual of 1–2 mg/l.
  • A drinking-water test is required at least every six months, and a sample should also be taken a week after the regular clean, a week after any emergency disinfection, after a failed result, after major pipework changes, and when a new water source is added.
  • The tank should be inspected internally at least monthly, and the cleaning and disinfection reports must be kept on file and produced on demand during a Municipality inspection.
  • Hot and cold pipework is disinfected at least twice a year to a free chlorine residual of 1–2 mg/l for one hour, with each outlet run for at least five minutes.

Higher-risk premises — schools, clinics, hospitals, food service — are expected to clean more often, typically quarterly. A second Municipality document (DM-HSD-GU44-LCWS2, version 3, issued 13 September 2022) applies the same six-month interval to Legionella control in hotels, schools, commercial and government buildings, residential buildings and labour accommodation.

The rule itself is not new. What is new is the enforcement. As one Dubai compliance guide put it in July 2026, "fines for non-compliance can reach AED 50,000" — and the Municipality's inspections now check the certificate date, not just whether the tank looks clean. Three separate guides on the rule were published or updated in the last two weeks alone (27 October to 2 November 2026), which is usually a sign that enforcement stories are circulating and owners are searching. For a villa owner, the practical rhythm is simple: clean in March and September, and one visit lands before the hottest months and one after. For an apartment tenant, the duty sits with the building owner or manager — but the tenant is the one drinking the water, so it is worth knowing whether the building is compliant.

What a proper clean actually includes

A compliant water tank cleaning is not someone peering through the hatch with a torch. It is a full empty, clean, disinfect and test. Here is what should happen, in order:

  1. Isolate and drain. The water supply to the tank is isolated, the tank is drained, and any sludge and sediment at the bottom is removed.
  2. Scrub the interior. Walls, floor and lid are scrubbed to remove biofilm — the slimy layer where bacteria grow — along with scale and sediment. The lid and overflow must be checked: a missing or broken lid is how dust, sand and insects get in.
  3. Disinfect. The tank is disinfected with chlorine at the Municipality's specified concentration (50 mg/L), left for the required contact time, then flushed.
  4. Refill and flush the pipework. The tank is refilled and the hot and cold pipework is disinfected, with each outlet run for at least five minutes.
  5. Test the water. A sample goes to an ISO 17025-accredited laboratory. The report should confirm the water is safe — including the free chlorine residual of 1–2 mg/l.
  6. Issue the certificate. You receive a cleaning certificate and the lab report. Keep both. They are what a Municipality inspector asks for, and they are what restarts the six-month clock.

A standard villa tank takes roughly two to three hours. If a company offers a "clean" with no certificate and no lab test, it has not done the compliant job — cleaning alone is not considered compliance. The test is the proof. Signs your tank needs attention now

You do not need to wait for the six-month date if the water is telling you something is wrong. Get the tank checked if you notice any of these:

  • The water tastes or smells off — musty, earthy or chemical. Taste and smell changes are the most common first sign.
  • Discoloured water — brown, yellow or cloudy water from a tap, especially first thing in the morning.
  • Sediment or grit in the water, or in the filter of a tap.
  • Low water pressure that is new — sediment can choke the tank outlet or the pump filter, and pressure loss is also the first symptom of a concealed leak elsewhere in the system. (See Al Hamra's guide on plumbing leaks and water pressure on alhamratechnical.com.)
  • The tank lid is missing, cracked or loose, or the overflow pipe is dripping constantly — an overflowing tank wastes water and keeps the tank open to contamination.
  • You have just moved in and nobody can show you a cleaning certificate dated within the last six months. A pre-move-in tank check belongs on the same list as the AC service and the lock change. The Healthy Home explains why to clean the tank before moving into a new UAE home.

If the water looks or tastes wrong, do not just run the tap and hope. Get the tank inspected and, if needed, tested — the lab result is the only answer that counts.

Who pays — owner, landlord or tenant?

This is the question that causes most arguments, and the answer has two parts: what Dubai Municipality says, and what tenancy law says.

Dubai Municipality guidance places the duty on the building owner or manager. The owner, principal manager or person in charge is the one who must arrange the cleaning and keep the records. For a villa you own, that is you. For an apartment in a tower, it is the building management or owners' association — ask them for the latest cleaning certificate and lab report. If they cannot produce one dated within six months, the building is overdue.

Tenancy law puts the starting duty on the landlord. Under Article 16 of Dubai Law No. 26 of 2007, unless the parties agree otherwise, the landlord is responsible during the lease for maintenance and for repairing any defect or damage that affects the tenant's intended use of the property. Article 17 makes the landlord answerable for defects, damage, deficiency and wear that are not the tenant's fault. Article 19 requires the tenant to maintain the property as an ordinary person would maintain their own — routine upkeep, not the building's systems.

In practice, most Dubai tenancy contracts add a minor-maintenance clause: the tenant pays for repairs under an agreed threshold — typically AED 500, sometimes AED 1,000 for villas — and the landlord pays above it. That clause is a contractual allocation, not a figure fixed by law, and it only works if it is in the Ejari-registered contract. A clause that caps the landlord's total yearly maintenance liability at AED 2,000, or forces the tenant to replace a failed chiller, would likely be struck down by the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre.

Applied to the tank: in a villa, the six-month clean is a landlord responsibility unless the contract says otherwise — it is a building system, not routine upkeep, and it costs around the minor-maintenance threshold or above. In a tower, it is the building's responsibility, not the individual tenant's. The tenant's job is to report: tell the landlord or building management in writing, keep a dated record and photos, and keep a copy of any certificate you are shown.

One warning from the legal guides: a landlord can refuse to pay for damage the tenant caused, but the claim must be supported by evidence. And a tenant cannot withhold rent over a repair — the route is a complaint to the Rental Dispute Settlement Centre, not a rent strike. Why November is the right month

November is the best month of the year to deal with the tank — and to deal with the air conditioning at the same time.

The tank reason is simple: the summer heat is over, the tank has spent five months baking, and the next six-month cycle should be set before winter, not after it. A clean done in November puts the next one in May, right before the hottest months — the March-and-September rhythm that Dubai compliance guides recommend, shifted to fit the winter window.

The air conditioning reason is the same logic. November to February is the best booking window for AC winter service in Dubai: the system that ran non-stop from May to October is carrying five months of wear — fouled coils, a half-blocked drain line, tired capacitors, a slow refrigerant leak — and maintenance companies are wide open right now, before the pre-summer rush. A winter visit should cover coil cleaning on both sides, a drain-line flush and pan clean, filter service, a refrigerant pressure check (a leak search, not just a top-up), an electrical health check with capacitor values metered, and thermostat calibration. (See Al Hamra's guides on winter AC maintenance in Dubai and AC servicing frequency on alhamratechnical.com.)

Book the two jobs together and you get one visit instead of two: the tank clean, the AC winter service, and — while the technician is already there — a check of the water heater before its only real season, and a look at any damp patch or ceiling stain that might be a concealed leak. Winter is also the best season for thermal leak detection, because cooler outside temperatures give the cameras the contrast they need to spot a hot-water pipe leaking inside a wall. One visit, four checks, and the house is set until the pre-summer service in March. That is the whole point of November.

What to ask before you book

Before any company touches your tank, ask these six questions. The answers tell you whether you are hiring a compliant contractor or a man with a brush.

  1. "Are you approved by Dubai Municipality for tank cleaning — and what is your establishment number?" The cleaning firm must hold DM approval. If they cannot give you a number, do not book.
  2. "Is the lab test included, and which laboratory does it?" The lab should be ISO 17025-accredited. The report is the proof the water is safe.
  3. "Will I receive a cleaning certificate?" You need the certificate and the lab report, kept on file. No certificate, no compliance.
  4. "What exactly is included — tank only, or pipework disinfection too?" The Municipality guideline covers hot and cold pipework as well as the tank. Get the scope in writing.
  5. "How long will the water be off?" A standard villa tank takes roughly two to three hours. Plan around it — fill a few containers first.
  6. "What happens if the test fails?" A proper contractor re-disinfects and re-tests. Ask before the work starts, not after a failed result.

If a quote is far below the published bands, ask what has been left out. A quote that skips the lab test and the certificate is not cheaper — it is incomplete.

Frequently asked questions

How often must a water tank be cleaned in Dubai?

At least every six months. Dubai Municipality's Technical Guidelines (DM-HSD-GU17-DW2) require every water tank to be emptied, cleaned and disinfected at least twice a year, with a drinking-water test at least every six months. The clock starts from the date of the last cleaning certificate.

What happens if I do not clean my water tank?

The Municipality can fine up to AED 50,000 for non-compliance, and inspectors check the certificate date. Beyond the fine, an uncleaned tank grows biofilm and sediment, which is what affects the taste, smell and safety of the water.

Who pays for water tank cleaning — landlord or tenant?

Dubai Municipality guidance places the duty on the building owner or manager. Under tenancy law (Law No. 26 of 2007, Article 16), the landlord is responsible for maintenance unless the contract says otherwise. In a villa, the six-month clean is normally the landlord's; in a tower, it is the building management's. Check your Ejari-registered contract for a minor-maintenance clause — typically the tenant pays repairs under about AED 500.

How long does a water tank clean take?

Roughly two to three hours for a standard villa tank, including draining, scrubbing, disinfection and flushing. The lab result comes later — a sample is also taken about a week after the clean.

Can I clean the water tank myself?

You can look at it, but the compliant job requires an approved contractor, disinfection at the specified chlorine concentration, and a lab test from an accredited laboratory. A DIY rinse produces no certificate, and no certificate means no compliance.

Is November a good month for water tank cleaning?

Yes. The summer heat is over, and a November clean sets the next one for May, before the hottest months. It also pairs naturally with the AC winter service — one visit, tank and AC together, before the pre-summer rush.
